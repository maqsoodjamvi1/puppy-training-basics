Puppy Training Schedule for Large Breeds: Joint-Safe Weekly Milestones from 8 to 20 Weeks

Bringing home a massive puppy like a Great Dane, German Shepherd, or Mastiff is an incredible experience, but it comes with a unique set of physical responsibilities. Unlike smaller breeds that bounce around without a second thought, large and giant breed puppies are growing heavy bone structures that take up to 18 to 24 months to fully fuse. When I brought home my Mastiff mix, my veterinarian gave me a piece of advice that changed my entire training approach: treat your puppy’s joints like wet concrete.

Traditional obedience advice often tells you to tire a puppy out with endless fetch and jumping. For a large breed, that approach can cause micro-fractures in growth plates. Your training schedule needs to prioritize mental exhaustion over physical wear-and-tear. Here is how I structured my weekly training milestones from 8 to 20 weeks to build a well-behaved dog while protecting those vulnerable joints.

Weeks 8 to 10: Building Soft Foundations on Soft Surfaces

During your puppy’s first couple of weeks home, training should happen strictly on carpeted floors, grass, or rubber mats. Avoid slick hardwood or tile floors where a clumsy 25-pound puppy can slip and strain a shoulder.

Keep physical exercise to short, controlled bursts. A good rule of thumb is a five-minute walk per month of age, twice a day. At 8 weeks, that is a literal 10-minute maximum outing.

  • House Training: Take your puppy out every 45 minutes to an hour while they are awake. Carry them down any flight of stairs to protect their elbows and hips from the impact.
  • Name Recognition and Focus: Say your puppy’s name, and the second they look at you, mark it with a “Yes!” and hand them a pea-sized piece of chicken. Do this in 2-minute sessions.
  • Surface Desensitization: Gently introduce your puppy to walking across cardboard boxes, yoga mats, and low-pile rugs to build body awareness without high-impact jumping.

Weeks 11 to 13: Impulse Control Without the Jumps

By week 11, your puppy is gaining coordination, but their joints are still soft. This is the prime window to introduce impulse control so you do not end up with a 100-pound dog that knocks you over at the front door.

Instead of teaching “Sit” by luring the treat over their head—which encourages them to jump up—I teach a “Sit” by holding the treat right at their chest and slowly moving it backward. This keeps all four paws firmly on the ground.

  • The Wait at Doors: Practice opening the front door only an inch. If your puppy rushes forward, close the door. Repeat until they sit patiently while the door swings wide open.
  • Gentle Food Taking: Teach a “Take it” and “Leave it” command using your kibble. If teeth touch your skin, immediately close your fist and say “Oops,” removing the reward.
  • Socialization via Car Rides: Instead of walking miles through public parks, sit in the trunk of your parked car with the hatch open. Feed your puppy high-value treats while watching distant dogs, bicycles, and skateboards pass by.

Weeks 14 to 17: Loose-Leash Walking and Low-Impact Conditioning

A large breed puppy pulling on a leash at 14 weeks is manageable; a pulling adolescent at 40 pounds is annoying; a pulling adult at 110 pounds is a safety hazard. Now is the time to cement loose-leash habits.

Switch from a standard collar to a front-clip harness. This redirects your puppy’s forward momentum without putting pressure on their delicate neck and trachea.

  • The Be-A-Tree Game: The moment the leash goes taut, stop walking completely. Stand like a statue. The second your puppy takes a step backward or looks at you, creating slack in the leash, praise and resume walking.
  • Crate Confidence: Practice closing the crate door for just 5 seconds while your puppy is inside with a stuffed, frozen Kong. Gradually build duration to prevent separation anxiety.
  • Target Touch: Teach your puppy to touch their nose to the palm of your hand on command (“Touch”). You can use this to guide them through narrow doorways or around obstacles without pulling their collar.

Weeks 18 to 20: Mental Workouts and Teenager Prep

At 18 to 20 weeks, your puppy is entering adolescence. They might start “forgetting” cues they mastered a month ago. Because their growth plates are still wide open, you still cannot take them jogging, running on pavement, or let them play rough with adult dogs.

Instead, double down on brain games. I feed my large breed dogs exclusively out of puzzle toys, snuffle mats, or by scattering their kibble through the backyard grass. Making them use their nose to hunt for food burns twice as much energy as a traditional walk.

  • Solidifying Recalls: Practice the “Come” command in a low-distraction hallway, working up to an enclosed yard. Always use a long line (a 15-foot light leash) if you are practicing recalls outside to ensure they cannot bolt.
  • Vet Handling Practice: Practice lifting your puppy’s paws, looking inside their ears, and gently holding their muzzle. A giant breed dog needs to be comfortable with a veterinarian or groomer handling their massive frame.
  • Place Command: Teach your puppy to go to a designated mat or dog bed and stay there while you eat dinner or cook, keeping them out from under your feet in the kitchen.

Navigating the puppy phase with a large breed requires patience and a constant focus on anatomical safety. By trading high-impact physical exercise for mental enrichment and steady, structured impulse control, you set your dog up for a structurally sound, well-mannered adulthood.
